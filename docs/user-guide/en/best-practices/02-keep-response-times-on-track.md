Overdue tickets build up quietly. A few steady habits keep them down.

> **Who can do this:** Supervisors and Admins.

## Watch the queue, not just the totals

Open the [live queue](../reports/live-queue.md) at the start of the day. Look at tickets waiting for a first reply and at the ones due soon. Sort the ticket list by SLA to work the soonest-due first. See [Find and sort tickets](../working-with-tickets/find-and-sort-tickets.md).

## Keep targets honest

Set targets by priority that your team can meet, and put your real hours in. The clock pauses outside them and on holidays. See [Response-time targets](../automation/sla-targets-and-policies.md) and [Business hours and holidays](../automation/business-hours-and-holidays.md).

## Share the work evenly

Set a sensible capacity per channel, and ask agents to set themselves On break rather than leave tickets sitting. See [Routing and agent availability](../automation/routing-and-agent-availability.md) and [Your status and account menu](../account-and-access/my-status-and-account.md).

## Park what is waiting, and close what is dead

- Use [snooze](../working-with-tickets/snooze-a-ticket.md) for tickets that wait on something, so they leave your list and come back by themselves.
- Turn on [auto-close](../automation/auto-close.md) for Pending tickets the customer never answers.

## Escalate early

Add a rule that raises the priority when the response-time clock runs out, and tell the team lead. See [Rules](../automation/rules.md).

## Check it worked

The number of open tickets past a target in the [team report](../reports/team-report.md) goes down week by week.

## Troubleshooting

| What you see | Why | What to do |
|--------------|-----|------------|
| Many tickets past SLA from the start | Targets are tighter than your staffing. | Loosen targets or add capacity. |
| Tickets sit unassigned | Nobody is Available or has room. | Review capacity and availability. |
